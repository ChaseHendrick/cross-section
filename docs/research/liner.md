## 1. Subject and date

**Subject:** RMS *Queen Mary*, Cunard White Star Line, built by John Brown & Co., Clydebank, as "Hull Number 534" (keel laid 1 December 1930, work halted December 1931, launched 26 September 1934, maiden voyage from Southampton 27 May 1936) [S1, S2]. She is a real, single, well-documented ship, so the drawing is **modelled on Queen Mary herself**, not a composite.

**Moment depicted:** a summer westbound crossing, Southampton to New York via Cherbourg [S55], in **August 1938**, on the third day out, in mid-Atlantic. That month she took the westbound Blue Riband, Bishop Rock to Ambrose Light, 4 to 8 August 1938, at 30.99 knots [S64]. Use 28.5 knots (her service speed [S1]) for normal animation and 31 knots as an optional "record run" mode.

**Why this ship and date:**
- She is preserved at Long Beach, California, and her 1936 fittings were described in detail at the time (quotations from *The Shipbuilder* and the 1936-37 part-work *Shipping Wonders of the World*) [S3, S4, S5, S12].
- 1938 sits after the 1936 teething fixes (handrails added after an October 1936 gale [S67]; engineers' extra quarters built on the Sports Deck in 1936-37 [S39]) and before war service (grey paint from 1940 [S1]).
- Original passenger lists of 1936-37 survive with the ship's printed "Information for Passengers" (meal hours, kennels, deck chairs) [S59, S60, S61].

**Composite elements to declare on the caption panel:** the room positions along the hull are layout estimates built from verified room sizes and deck order (section 3). People are fictional. Some routines are period practice rather than Queen Mary records, and are flagged.

**Class names in 1936-39 (important):** the top class was called **Cabin Class** (effectively first class), the second class **Tourist Class**, and the lowest **Third Class**. After the war these became First, Cabin and Tourist [S17, S75]. Capacity: 776 Cabin, 785 Tourist, 579 Third, total 2,140 [S1]. Cabin Class lives amidships (its Main Lounge is "amidships on promenade deck" [S22]), Tourist aft (its smoking room is at the after end of the Promenade Deck and its pool "at the back of the ship" [S45, S25]), Third forward (its entrance, stairs and public rooms are "in the forward part" from Main deck down to E deck [S19]).

## 3. Cutaway composition

**Viewing side:** look at the **port side**, so the **bow is at the left** (x = 0) and the stern at the right (x = 310.7). The ship then steams leftward, which reads as "westbound" on a map. Several documented features are on the port side and so read best: the kennels on the Sports Deck [S9], the gymnasium on the Sun Deck [S24], the wireless transmitting room [S26], the Long Gallery [S43] and the crew working alleyway [S28].

**What is cut away:** the port shell plating and port half of each deck are removed down the centreline, from keel to compass platform, for the full length. Leave the hull outline, the funnels (cut in half to show uptakes), and a strip of black hull plating at both ends (forecastle, stern) to frame the picture. Keep the lifeboats on the far (starboard) side visible as a row seen through the cut, and draw a few port boats "lifted away" above their davits for the classic exploded feel.

**Aspect ratio:** the ship is about 310.7 m long and 71 m to the masthead, with the sea below. Suggested canvas about **4:1** (for example 6400 x 1600 px) covering x from -10 to 320 m and y from -8 (dark water below the keel, cosmetic) to 76 m. Sky above, sea filling the lower part, waterline at y = 11.8.

**Exterior colours:** hull black above the waterline, red boot-topping at and below the waterline, white upperworks and bulwarks [S53]. Funnels "Cunard red", a red-orange, with black tops [S52, S53] and thin black bands at the segment joints (count was not consistent on early ships; draw two on each funnel) [S54]. Teak decks [S38]. Lifeboat covers buff/tan [S11]. Three-flight terraced decks at the stern **[unverified, illustrative]**.

## 6. Machinery and moving parts

### 6a. Propulsion chain (steam path)

1. **Fuel:** oil fuel, about 6,300 tons carried [S4]. Heated and pumped by electric rotary pumps to the burners [S3].
2. **Boilers:** 24 Yarrow water-tube main boilers in four boiler rooms (6 per room), 7 burners each (168 burners), steam at 400 psi [S1] (425 psi per S3) superheated to 700°F (371°C). Plus 3 Scotch boilers in No. 1 boiler room for hotel services: 27 boilers in 5 rooms [S3, S7]. Forced-draught fans totalling 3,000 hp [S3].
3. **Steam pipes:** 2,600 ft of main steam piping; ahead pipes 16 in bore, astern 12.5 in [S3].
4. **Turbines:** four sets, one per shaft. Each set has one high-pressure, two intermediate-pressure and one low-pressure turbine; astern turbines built in, 16 ahead and 8 astern in total [S3]. Steam passes HP > IP > IP > LP > condenser.
5. **Condensers:** four, each 28 ft high, 13,780 copper-nickel tubes, 41,000 sq ft, vacuum 29 in of mercury [S3]. Water returns to the boilers as feed.
6. **Gearing:** single-reduction double-helical gears; each gear-wheel centre 51.5 tons; each turbine set about 70 ft long overall [S1, S3]. Turbine rotor speed **[unverified]**: animate pinions several times faster than the big wheel.
7. **Shafts:** forward engine room drives the two outer (wing) shafts, after engine room the two inner shafts; total shafting 1,024 ft [S3, S48].
8. **Propellers:** four-bladed bronze by J. Stone and Co., 35 tons, nearly 20 ft, pitch 18 ft 2 in [S58]; later sets 18.5 ft, 32 tons [S6]; 22 sets used over 31 years [S6].
9. **Power:** 160,000 shp normal [S2, S7]; 200,000 shp quoted as maximum [S1, S3]; 212,000 shp maximum with 200 rpm per propeller [S1].

## 7. Environment

- **Route and position:** Southampton, Cherbourg, then the open Atlantic; westbound record distance measured Bishop Rock to Ambrose Light [S64]. The restaurant map showed a southern winter/spring track (to avoid icebergs) and a summer/autumn track [S1]. In August use the summer track.
- **Weather:** August mid-Atlantic: moderate swell, scattered cumulus, long low light at evening. Near the Grand Banks fog is common where the cold Labrador Current meets the Gulf Stream, and icebergs occur [S57]. Optional "fog bank" event (fog on the maiden voyage prevented a record attempt [S55]).
- **Sea state option:** she was a heavy roller [S67] until stabilisers were fitted in 1958 [S55]; one early crossing rolled to 44 degrees; heavy furniture and a piano broke loose; 25,000 pieces of china and glass broken a year in early years [S67]. For animation, roll amplitude 2-6 degrees, period 10-15 s **[illustrative]**.
- **Light cycle:** clocks go back one hour at midnight [S62], so a westbound "day" lasts 25 hours: a nice gag for the clock UI.
- **Sky at night:** the forward funnel was floodlit from the compass platform [S41].
- **Sounds (for optional audio):**
  - Ship's whistle: three whistles tuned to 55 Hz (the A one octave above the lowest piano note) [S1]; heard ten miles away [S10].
  - Ship's bell: struck each half hour, pairs of strokes up to eight bells per watch [S65].
  - Propeller beat: 4 blades x 3 rev/s = 12 Hz throb (computed), heard as vibration in the stern [S75].
  - Engine and boiler rooms: roar of 3,000 hp of fans [S3], hiss of burners, whine of turbines.
  - Wireless room: Morse key and typewriters [S26].
  - Kitchens: dough mixer, potato peeler, dishwashing machines, crockery [S16].
  - Promenade: wind through sliding windows, deck-chair creaks, tea trolley [S38].
  - Public rooms: piano, dance band, applause; tea-dance in ballroom [S44].
  - Kennels: barking near the second funnel [S9].
  - Crew bar: darts thudding [S15].

## 10. Pitfalls (avoid these)

1. **No grey paint.** The grey "Grey Ghost" scheme is wartime, from 1940 [S1].
2. **Class names.** In 1936-39 the top class is "Cabin", not "First"; "Tourist" is second [S17].
3. **No coal stokers shovelling.** Main and auxiliary boilers are oil-fired [S3]. Firemen tend burners. (The only coal is the Smoking Room fire [S36].)
4. **No radar.** The compass platform as built held a direction-finder, standard compass, funnel floodlight and searchlight [S41]; the radar room seen at Long Beach is a later addition [S8, S41].
5. **No stabilisers.** Fitted only in 1958 [S55]. She should roll.
6. **No chapel.** Christian services were held in the Main Lounge [S22]; the "Wedding Chapel" at Long Beach is modern [S7]. The synagogue is genuine [S14].
7. **Long Beach deck names are post-war.** "R deck" was C deck in 1936 [S47].
8. **No Mermaid Bar.** It was a post-war addition [S38]. In the source used, shuffleboard is mentioned only for the post-war ship [S29], so leave it out.
9. **Funnel colour.** Red-orange "Cunard red" with black top and thin black bands, not White Star buff [S54]. The present funnels at Long Beach are replicas [S1].
10. **Propellers.** Four-bladed in 1936-37 sources [S4, S58]; claims that the originals were three-bladed are not supported by those sources.
11. **Numbers that vary by source:** gross tonnage 80,774 or 81,237; power 160,000 normal, 200,000-212,000 maximum; boiler pressure 400 or 425 psi; crew 1,100, 1,101 or 1,174. Quote one and cite it.
12. **Bow on the left means port side visible.** Do not mirror starboard features (playroom, Starboard Gallery) onto the near side.
13. **Women crew:** stewardesses are documented aboard [S67]; women deck or engineer officers would be anachronistic for a 1930s British liner **[unverified, period norm]**.
14. **Not the Lindbergh dogs westbound:** they travelled to Britain [S9].
15. **Ventilation:** Tourist cabins were ventilated by Thermotank punkah louvres [S31]; draw round louvre nozzles, not modern air-conditioning grilles.
