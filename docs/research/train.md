# Research dossier: 1930s British steam express (scene id `train`)

Prepared for the scene illustrator. World units are metres. Every claim is tied to a source key (S-numbers, listed with full URLs in section 11) or flagged **[unverified, illustrative]**. Where sources disagree, both values are given. The single most valuable source is S1, the LNER's own 1937 brochure for the train (16 pages, scanned page by page; I read every page, including the seat plan and the point-to-point timetable). S2 is a 1937-38 engineering part-work chapter written while the train was new.

---

## 1. Subject and date

**Subject:** the LNER streamlined express **"The Coronation"**, King's Cross to Edinburgh Waverley, on its **northbound ("down") run**, hauled by Gresley Class A4 Pacific **No. 4489 *Dominion of Canada***. This is a real, single, well-documented train, not a composite. Only the people are invented.

**Moment:** **Thursday 29 July 1937**, the fourth week of service (the train began on Monday 5 July 1937 and ran on weekdays except Saturdays [S1]). The "hero" frame is **9.05 pm, crossing the Royal Border Bridge at Berwick-upon-Tweed**, the booked passing time at Berwick [S1]. An optional "speed" frame is about **4.40 pm between Hitchin and Huntingdon**, where the booked average is 85.1 mph, the fastest on the run [S1].

**Why this train, date and engine:**
- The LNER's 1937 brochure gives the exact formation (a numbered seat plan, London end to Edinburgh end), timings to the half minute, fares, the meal tariff and the dimensions [S1]. S2 independently confirms the order of cars "from the north end" and their seat counts. This removes almost all guesswork about the consist.
- Late July 1937 is inside the first summer season, when the "beaver-tail" observation car ran at the rear [S1, S3].
- 4489 is the engine drawn on the brochure cover [S1] and the engine of the 30 June 1937 test run (109½ mph with 320 tons) [S2]. Its whistle was "specially sent from Canada by the Canadian Pacific Railway Company" [S1]. In July 1937 it still had a single chimney [S31] and its side valances [S31].
- Choosing 1937 rather than 1938 keeps the scene clear of later changes: Mallard's 126 mph record (3 July 1938) [S13], the 60 mph limit at Wiske Moor troughs (after 8 October 1937) [S10], the extra smokebox casing doors fitted from April 1938 [S31], and 4489's Canadian-pattern bell (fitting date uncertain, see section 10).

## 3. Cutaway composition

**Viewing side and direction.** Look at the **west (left-hand) side** of the northbound train, so the engine is at the **left (x = 0)** and the train runs **leftwards**. Reasons: the A4 driver's seat is on the left [unverified for A4 specifically; the LNER built A3s new with left-hand drive, S34], so the driver is on the near side at his window; the tender corridor runs along the **right-hand side** [S9], so the cutaway reveals it on the far wall; and at 9.05 pm the low sun is in the west-north-west (computed azimuth about 303°, altitude about 1°, see section 7), behind the viewer, lighting the near side of the train orange while the eastern sky beyond (over the Tweed estuary and the North Sea) deepens to blue.

**What is cut away.** Remove the near (west) half of every vehicle down the centreline, from the rail to the roof, for the full 178 m. Keep a thin strip of blue body side below the floor and the skirts, keep the roof profile as a silhouette line, and keep the far-side wall, windows and fittings visible. On the engine, cut the streamlined casing, boiler and firebox in half to show tubes, flues and the fire; keep the near-side wheels and motion drawn in front (as the classic books do) so the mechanism reads.

**Aspect ratio.** The object is 178 m long and only 4 m tall. Use a **horizontally pannable panorama about 9:1** (for example 9000 x 1000 px for x = -12 to 190 m, y = -8 to 14 m), with the bridge parapet and the tops of the arches along the bottom and the piers dropping out of frame. Alternative: a two-tier layout, top tier engine + Twins A and B (x 0 to 91.6), bottom tier Twins C, D and the observation car (x 91.6 to 178).

**Exploded slices.** Natural, structurally honest boundaries are the gaps between vehicles: **x = 14.6** (engine/tender drawbar), **21.65** (tender/Twin A), **56.6** (A/B), **91.6** (B/C), **126.6** (C/D), **161.6** (D/observation car). That gives six boundaries and seven slices. Each articulated twin must stay in one piece: its two bodies share a centre bogie [S2].

## 5. Animals and small life

- **Dogs.** Railways advertised cheap travel for dogs ("Take your Dog with you by Rail") and water could be had at refreshment rooms or from staff [S22]. Dogs, guns and fishing tackle went north in summer for the shooting season [S21]. Draw one spaniel in the front guard's van (Z18) on a lead with a water bowl. Whether dogs on the Coronation travelled in the van or with owners is **[unverified]**.
- **Cats.** A cat at the engine shed or at York station is plausible period colour **[illustrative]**; never inside the streamliner.
- **Lineside livestock [illustrative]:** sheep on the Northumberland and Berwickshire uplands, dairy cattle in the Vale of York, heavy horses with hay carts (horses still did much farm work in 1937).
- **Rabbits** on embankments **[illustrative]**.
- **Birds [illustrative]:** rooks over fields, gulls over the Tweed estuary and coast, pigeons under the King's Cross and York roofs. Mute swans on the Tweed at Berwick are a well-known colony today; their presence in 1937 is **[unverified]**.
- **Salmon** in the nets of the Tweed fishermen below the bridge [S28].
- **Do not** add rats, pit ponies or horses aboard: nothing in the sources puts them on this train.

## 6. Machinery and moving parts

**Speeds to animate (from S1, S2):** booked average 65.5 mph overall, 71.9 mph to York [S1]; booked 85.1 mph Hitchin to Huntingdon, 68.1 mph Belford to Berwick [S1]; slowings to 20 mph at Peterborough and 30 mph over Selby swing bridge [S2]; in service the up train touched 106 mph near Werrington on 3 August 1937 [S2]; 4489 reached 109½ mph on its 30 June test [S2]. For the hero frame use **60 mph (26.8 m/s)** across the bridge **[illustrative; S1 books 68.1 mph average for the section, the actual speed over the bridge is not documented]**.

**Rotation rates (computed from verified wheel diameters):**

| Speed | m/s | Driving wheels (2.032 m) | Bogie (0.965 m) | Tender (1.27 m) | Exhaust beats per second (6 per revolution) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 20 mph | 8.9 | 84 rpm | 177 rpm | 134 rpm | 8.4 |
| 60 mph | 26.8 | 252 rpm | 531 rpm | 403 rpm | 25.2 |
| 72 mph | 32.2 | 303 rpm | 637 rpm | 484 rpm | 30.3 |
| 90 mph | 40.2 | 378 rpm | 796 rpm | 605 rpm | 37.8 |
| 109½ mph | 49.0 | 460 rpm | 969 rpm | 736 rpm | 46.0 |

Six beats per revolution is the arithmetic of three double-acting cylinders; Gresley engines give "a regular, distinctive exhaust beat" [S15]. Above about 20 beats a second the ear hears a purr, not separate chuffs [S8 describes the "bark" quietening "to a purr"].

**Engine mechanism.**
- **Pistons and rods.** Stroke 26 in (0.66 m) [S1]. Outside connecting rods drive the middle coupled axle **[illustrative]**; coupling rods link all three coupled wheels. Mean piston speed at 90 mph is about 8.3 m/s (computed).
- **Valve gear.** Walschaerts outside; the inside valve is worked by Gresley's conjugating levers, "an adding machine" summing the two outside valve positions [S15]. Cranks at roughly 120° [S15]. Animate the expansion link rocking once per wheel turn, the radius rod sliding in it, the combination lever swinging.
- **Cut-off.** Long cut-off (about 75%) at starting, 15-18% at speed [S8, S2]: draw the radius rod near the link centre at speed.
- **Fire.** Fireman feeds coal "under the door, in each back corner, down the sides ... and in the front corners", keeping the fire saucer-shaped [S8]. The tender holds enough coal for the whole run [S2].
- **Water.** Exhaust injector keeps the boiler fed [S8]. Six trough sets on the route, at roughly 27, 79, 121, 145, 221 and 319 miles from King's Cross (cumulative from the spacing table in S12): Langley (south of Stevenage), Werrington (north of Peterborough), Muskham (north of Newark), Scrooby (south of Bawtry), Wiske Moor (milepost 32 from York [S10]) and Lucker (between Alnmouth and Berwick) [S11]. At 60 mph a scoop could take 2,000 gallons in 20 seconds [S11]; troughs held about 5 in of water with only the top 2 in skimmed [S11]. Crews reported the Wiske Moor pick-up sometimes insufficient to reach Lucker [S10]. Approximate passing times on the down run, interpolated from the S1 timetable: Langley 4.26, Werrington 5.06, Muskham 5.40, Scrooby 6.00, Wiske Moor 7.11, Lucker 8.49 pm **[computed]**.
- **Smoke and steam.** The wedge front throws air upward "to catch the column of exhaust steam ... and carry it well up over the driver's cab" [S2]. Draw a mostly white-grey plume lying back over the train, darker for a few seconds after firing.
- **Safety valves** lift with a roar when pressure peaks (S8 describes blowing off at starting).
- **Brakes.** Vacuum brake [S4]; brake cylinders under each vehicle.
- **Whistle.** Chime whistle on A4s (the Silver Jubilee's was famous at York) [S5]; 4489's whistle came from the Canadian Pacific [S1].

**Train mechanism.**
- **Articulation.** Centre bogies swivel under both body ends on curves [S2]; rubber fairings flex [S1, S2].
- **Generators.** Axle-driven, belt-turned dynamos supplying 32 kW for cooking, lighting, ventilation and refrigeration [S1].
- **Ventilation fans.** Full air change every three minutes [S1]; show a faint shimmer at floor inlets and roof grilles.
- **Kitchens.** Electric ranges, refrigerator, water-boiler [S1, S6]; steam from pans and urns.
- **Rail joints.** With 60 ft rails **[illustrative]**, each wheel crosses about 2.2 joints a second at 90 mph, 1.5 at 60 mph.
- **Observation car.** Not articulated; two ordinary bogies [S2].

**Outside.** Semaphore arms dropping or rising as the train approaches; signal box levers; telegraph wires appearing to rise and fall between poles (parallax); the coble rowing and the net drawing in [S28]; hay carts; gates at crossings.

## 9. Hidden details and stories

1. **The other Coronation.** At about 7.17 pm, between Northallerton and Darlington, the up train (left Edinburgh 4.30) flashes past in the opposite direction, beaver tail last. Times computed from the two booked timetables [S1]: *documented timetable, computed meeting point.*
2. **Last month's news.** On a newspaper in third class, a headline about the 30 June test run at 109½ mph by Dominion of Canada [S2]: *documented event, invented reader.*
3. **The stopwatch boy.** A schoolboy at a vestibule window times quarter-mile posts with a stopwatch and pencils speeds into a notebook: *plausible invention, a well-known hobby of the period.*
4. **Damping the coal.** The fireman turns a small hose on the coal to stop dust blowing about [S8]: *documented practice.*
5. **Calling the signals.** Driver and fireman both point and shout "Off!" at a distant signal [S8]: *documented practice.*
6. **Not a rattle.** An attendant sets down a full tray at 70 mph; the flat-handled cutlery lies still [S2]: *documented design, invented moment.*
7. **The shilling hour.** In the observation car the attendant gently ends a session; one passenger pays another shilling to stay [S1 for the charge; S30 says sessions had a 15-minute break]: *documented rule, invented moment.*
8. **A spaniel in the van.** The guard shares a biscuit with a gun dog among trunks and rod cases [S21, S22]: *plausible invention.*
9. **Telegram at York.** The train attendant hands a reporter's telegram to a York porter during the three-minute stop [S1 for the service]: *plausible invention.*
10. **Spray at the troughs.** At Lucker a white fan of water bursts behind the tender scoop and spatters the windows of the first coach [S12 notes spray can drench leading vehicles]: *documented hazard, invented moment.*
11. **The salmon haul.** Below the bridge, a coble completes its half-circle as the train crosses; a boy on the bank waves his cap [S28]: *documented practice, invented moment.*
12. **Coat of arms.** The crest of Canada on the cab side, catching the low sun [S1]: *documented.*
13. **Lost tooth.** A little girl in third shows her new gap to an attendant: *invention.*
14. **Platelayer stands clear.** A ganger leans on his shovel in the cess as 312 tons of blue rush past: *plausible invention.*

## 10. Pitfalls

- **Wrong train mixtures.** Do not mix in the LMS *Coronation Scot* (Euston to Glasgow, blue with silver bands, ordinary square-ended coaches) [S2], or the silver-grey *Silver Jubilee* [S6], or *Flying Scotsman* teak stock and its cocktail bar and hairdresser [S7].
- **Livery.** Coaches are two-tone blue, not teak and not silver [S1]. Garter blue on A4s was special to the Coronation engines in July 1937; it became the class standard only from October 1937 [S3], so any other A4 passing should be green or grey **[colour of a specific passing engine unverified]**.
- **Chimney.** 4489 had a single chimney in 1937; double chimneys came with Mallard (March 1938) and were fitted to the rest in 1957-58 [S31, S14].
- **Valances.** Keep the side skirts over the wheels; they were removed in 1941-42 [S31].
- **Bell.** Do not draw the Canadian-pattern bell on 4489 in July 1937: an LNER Encyclopedia forum dates its fitting to March 1938 **[unverified]**; Wikipedia confirms it was steam-operated and in use by August 1939 [S14].
- **Numbers and emblems.** Use LNER numbers (4489) and stainless "L N E R" lettering [S1]; never BR numbers (60010) or BR emblems.
- **Observation car shape.** Draw the 1937 beaver tail with sloping glazed tail and "CORONATION" across the back [S2]; not the 1959 BR rebuild with bigger, more angled windows [S3, S38].
- **Formation.** Observation car always at the rear; the eight-car set is not turned [S2]. Kitchens are in two kitchen thirds, not a separate dining car [S1].
- **No coal-fired kitchen chimneys** on the kitchen cars: cooking is electric [S1].
- **Windows.** Air-conditioned; "in the hottest weather the windows may be kept closed" [S2]. Do not show heads out of windows.
- **Stops.** Down train stops only at York; do not show it stopping at Newcastle, Berwick or Dunbar [S1].
- **Days.** No Saturday running in 1937 [S1].
- **Class names.** First and Third; there is no "Second" [S1].
- **Speeds.** 126 mph is Mallard's 1938 test record with a dynamometer car [S13], not a service speed. Typical maxima were 90 mph downhill [S2], occasional 100+ [S2].
- **Route.** The Forth Bridge is not on this route; the train ends at Edinburgh Waverley [S1 map].
- **Era details.** No ballpoint pens, no plastic cups **[illustrative general knowledge]**; the Edinburgh Festival did not yet exist (founded 1947) **[unverified here]**, so the actress tours an ordinary theatre.
- **Smoke.** Not a permanent black cloud: the plume lifts over the cab [S2].
- **Tender corridor side.** Right-hand side [S9]; the driver is on the left (unverified for A4, see caption 44).
- **Troughs.** The 60 mph Wiske Moor limit dates from after 8 October 1937 [S10]; in July 1937 it did not yet exist.
