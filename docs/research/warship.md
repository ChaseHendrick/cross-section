## 1. Subject and date

**Subject:** HMS *Victory*, 104-gun first-rate ship of the line, designed by Sir Thomas Slade, built at Chatham and floated out on 7 May 1765 [S1, S9]. She is a single, real, exceptionally well documented ship, preserved in dry dock at Portsmouth by the National Museum of the Royal Navy (NMRN) [S1, S4]. The drawing is **modelled on Victory herself**, not a composite.

**Default moment: Wednesday 9 October 1805, at sea about 50 nautical miles west of Cadiz.** Nelson joined the fleet off Cadiz on the evening of 28 September [S8] and kept it out of sight of land, "sixteen to eighteen leagues West of Cadiz" [S27], "about fifty or sixty miles westward" [S8]. His diary for 9 October reads "Fresh breezes Easterly", wind westerly at night [S28]. That day he sent Collingwood his "Plan of Attack" [S28], and from 8 to 10 October transports were being cleared of bread and wine for the fleet [S27, S28]. Wednesday is a meatless ration day (oatmeal, butter, cheese, pease) [S18].

**Optional "beat to quarters" moment: Monday 21 October 1805 (Trafalgar), 11:50 to 13:15.** Light westerly breezes with "a long heavy swell" [S8]. The enemy opened fire on Victory at 11:50; she opened fire at four minutes past twelve; Nelson was hit at about 13:15 and died at 16:30 [S8, S1].

**Why this subject and date:**
- Victory's 1805 form is fixed by the 1800 to 1803 "Great Repair": closed stern galleries, copper-lined magazine, 104 guns, new figurehead, ochre and black paint [S1]. She survives to be measured.
- Her own surgeon, William Beatty, published a first-hand account in 1807 with times, weather, the cockpit, the middle deck and the admiral's habits [S8]. Nelson's October 1805 letters survive in print [S27, S28]. Her 1805 muster is in The National Archives [S7].
- October 1805 gives normal blockade life by default, with an exactly dated battle state as an option.

**Composite elements to declare on the caption panel:** room positions along the hull are layout estimates built from verified dimensions and deck order. All clickable people are fictional. Some routines are documented Royal Navy practice rather than Victory-specific records, and are flagged. Real people (Nelson, Captain Hardy and others) may appear only as non-clickable background figures.

**1805 terminology:** say **larboard**, not "port" (Beatty writes "larboard tack", "larboard guns" [S8]). The two watches are starboard and larboard.

## 3. Cutaway composition

**Orientation:** bow at the **left**, heading left; we look at the **larboard** side with the larboard half cut away on the centreline from keel to poop, so we see the inside of the starboard side (same convention as `liner`).

**Origin:** x = 0 is the stem at lower-gun-deck height; the gun deck runs x 0 to 56.7 [S1]. Figurehead and beakhead reach about x -5, the bowsprit end about x -10.3 (giving 69.3 m bowsprit to taffrail [S1]), the jib-boom about x -24, the taffrail about x 59 **[layout estimate]**. The keel runs about x 7.5 to 54.0 (46.5 m [S1]); the stem curves up and forward, the sternpost rakes slightly aft **[layout estimate]**.

**Cut convention:** remove the larboard shell and the larboard half of every deck and bulkhead. Draw centreline items (masts, capstans, pumps, hatches, wheel, stove) whole. Show cut planks and beam ends as a hatched strip along the section edge.

**Guns read breech-on:** starboard guns point away from us through ports in the far wall. Draw a stubby trapezoid carriage on four small wooden wheels ("trucks") with the round cascabel knob toward us, and behind it a square of daylight (open port) or the dark inner face of a shut lid. Slice faces in the exploded view show guns in profile.

**Bottom to top:** hold (casks, ballast, magazine forward), orlop (stores, cable tiers, cockpit aft), three gun decks of messes and guns, then forecastle (left), open waist, quarterdeck and poop (right). Masts at fore x 8.0, main x 30.5, mizzen x 45.0 **[layout estimate]**.

**Framing:** whole ship about 84 m wide (x -24 to 60) by 77 m tall (y -3 to 74), aspect about 1.1:1. Suggested world canvas 90 x 80 m, default **hull panorama** x -12 to 61, y -2 to 22 (about 3:1), rig revealed by panning up.

**Rig in side view:** yards cross the ship, so a true elevation shows them end-on. Follow picture-book convention: brace yards round 30 to 40 degrees so each square sail is a foreshortened trapezoid 55 to 70 per cent of its true width **[illustrative]**.

## 4. People

### 4a. Totals (verified)

- **820** on the Trafalgar muster besides Nelson and Hardy [S6]; 820 [S2, S4]: 9 commissioned officers, 21 midshipmen, 77 warrant and petty officers, 31 boys, the rest able and ordinary seamen, landsmen and supernumeraries [S2]; plus **146 Royal Marines** of the Chatham Division [S2] (a secondary marine history gives 123 [S24]).
- At least **22 nationalities** [S5]: English, Irish, Scottish, Welsh, Manx, American, French, German, Italian, Portuguese, Swedish, Danish, Norwegian, Dutch, Swiss, Canadian, Brazilian, West Indian, Jamaican, African, Indian, Maltese [S6].
- Oldest man: the purser, 69 [S54]. Casualties at Trafalgar: 57 killed, 102 wounded [S1, S9].
- **Women:** The National Archives names Jane Townshend (in HMS *Defiance*) as "the only woman positively identified as having served at Trafalgar" [S7, S15]. For Victory there is only a reported case: Mary Buick, said by local accounts to have nursed the wounded and helped prepare Nelson's body [S16]. Any woman aboard Victory is an optional, flagged figure (cast 50).

## 5. Animals and small life

- **Livestock (documented practice):** "cattle, sheep, pigs, goats, geese, hens and chickens to provide fresh meat, milk and eggs" [S17]; RMG names cattle, pigs, goats, chickens [S30]. Officers and men bought extra livestock [S63]. Nelson's standing orders had captains buy "bullocks, fresh beef, lemons, onions" [S28]. Draw hen coops on the poop, sheep and a goat penned in the waist, perhaps one bullock **[placement illustrative]**.
- **Ship's cat:** seamen "often kept cats and dogs as pets or ship's mascots", partly against rats [S30]; a 1754 account describes a ship heaving to for a kitten overboard [S31]. One cat on the orlop and in the bread room **[illustrative for Victory]**.
- **Dogs:** officers' dogs are documented on other ships [S31]; optional spaniel in the wardroom **[illustrative]**.
- **Rats:** the copper magazine lining was meant to keep them out [S1]; "rats carried disease" [S13]. Several in bilge, hold and bread room.
- **Weevils** in biscuit **[illustrative]**.
- **Outside:** sailors recorded whales, porpoises and seabirds [S30]; draw gulls, shearwaters and dolphins **[species illustrative]**.
- **Avoid:** parrots and monkeys belong to tropical voyages [S31], not a Cadiz blockade.

## 7. Environment

- **Place:** open Atlantic about 50 nautical miles west of Cadiz, land out of sight [S8, S27]. Frigates watch the port inshore, with ships of the line between to repeat signals [S8].
- **Neighbours:** Nelson had 23 sail of the line about 6 October [S27] and 27 by Trafalgar [S8]. Draw ochre-and-black two- and three-deckers at various ranges, a frigate, and a merchant-built **transport** alongside unloading bread and wine. An emptied transport hoists her ensign at the masthead to take back "empty casks and staves, and condemned provisions" [S28].
- **Weather:** 9 October "Fresh breezes Easterly", westerly at night [S28]; moderate sea, autumn sun, scattered cumulus **[illustrative]**. 21 October: light westerly airs, long heavy swell [S8]. From the evening of 22 October, a south-westerly gale [S8].
- **Colours:** Atlantic blue-grey #2F5E7A with white crests **[illustrative]**.
- **Night:** fleet signals by guns, "false fires and blue lights" [S8]; stern and top lanterns **[illustrative]**.
- **Sounds:** bell every half hour [S46]; boatswain's calls, no singing at work [S49]; creaking hull and rigging; wind in the shrouds; flapping canvas; clanking chain pumps; scraping holystones; hens and a goat; adze and mallet; marine drum; distant signal guns [S8]. Battle: broadsides, musketry, splintering, shouting, water hissing from the firemen's buckets [S8]. Bands playing "Rule Britannia" and "Britons Strike Home" at Trafalgar are reported in a contemporary narrative not opened here **[unverified]**.

## 10. Pitfalls

1. **No eye patch on Nelson**: he wore "a green shade over his forehead" [S8]; right sleeve empty.
2. **Brandy, not rum**: Nelson's body was kept in brandy, topped up with spirit of wine at Gibraltar [S8].
3. **Larboard, not port**, in 1805 labels [S8].
4. **No sailors' uniform**: ratings wore varied slops until 1857 [S21]. No square collars or "sailor suits".
5. **Marines' facings blue, not white**, after 1802 [S22]; glazed round hat, not a tall shako [S20].
6. **Pale ochre, not bright lemon**: after 2015 paint research the preserved ship was repainted a paler yellow believed closer to 1805 [S11]; black port lids [S11].
7. **Stern galleries closed in** during 1800 to 1803 [S1]: no open stern walk.
8. **Square beakhead bulkhead with heads**, not the rounded bow of 1814 [S1].
9. **Figurehead**: the simpler 1801 to 1803 design, not the 1765 four-continents group [S1].
10. **Armament**: 32-pdrs on the lower deck in 1805 (not 42-pdrs), 68-pdr carronades on the forecastle, 104 guns [S1, S2].
11. **Mast hoops yellow** [S8]; standing rigging tarred dark, running rigging natural **[illustrative but standard]**.
12. **No quarterdeck skylight**: Nelson had it removed [S1].
13. **Channels above the upper-deck ports**, moved there in 1800 to 1803 [S1].
14. **Steering**: double wheel on the quarterdeck, tiller ropes to a tiller below [S8]; no wheel on the poop.
15. **No keelhauling or plank-walking**: keelhauling belongs to 17th-century accounts [S64]; flogging was the usual punishment [S13, S20].
16. **No work songs**: the boatswain's pipe coordinated work [S49].
17. **Women**: do not present women as documented members of Victory's crew; only Jane Townshend (Defiance) is positively identified at Trafalgar [S7].
18. **Powder boys** were not only boys: women and older men also did the job [S41].
19. **No open flame near powder**: the magazine is lit from a separate light room **[illustrative but standard]**.
20. **Present-day museum features**: no dry dock, steel props, walkways, scaffolding, struck topmasts (2011) or removed lower mainmast (2021) [S1].
21. **Galley on the middle gun deck** [S61], not under the forecastle as in two-deckers.
22. **Cadiz not visible** on 9 October: the fleet kept about 50 miles offshore [S8, S27].
